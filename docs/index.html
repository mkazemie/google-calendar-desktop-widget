Two modes

Two modes, one button

In widget mode the title bar shrinks to just its two buttons and everything else lets your clicks through. The mouse button brings back a full, editable calendar in one click.

Click-through

Invisible to your mouse, even right-click

Right-click your wallpaper and Windows answers, not the calendar. The desktop context menu opens straight through it.

Live-wallpaper mode

Make Google Calendar your live wallpaper

Turn on Sit behind desktop icons and, in widget mode, your calendar slides under the desktop icons, just like a wallpaper. The icons stay on top, readable and clickable.

Behind your icons

Your schedule becomes part of the desktop itself. Files and shortcuts sit on top of it.

Edge shadows

Dark fades on the left and right edges keep icon labels legible. Set each side with one two-handle slider.

Still one click away

The title-bar buttons stay above the icons, so a fully editable calendar is always one click away.

Light & dark

FAQ

Questions, answered

How do I put Google Calendar on my Windows desktop?

Download and run the free installer, sign in to your Google account once, then click the mouse button in the widget's title bar. Your real Google Calendar stays on the desktop as a transparent, click-through widget, pinned beneath your other windows.

Can Google Calendar be my desktop wallpaper?

Yes. In Settings, turn on "Sit behind desktop icons (live wallpaper)". In widget mode the calendar then sits under your desktop icons like a live wallpaper, and edge shadows keep the icons readable on top of it.

Is it really free?

Yes. It's free and open source under the GNU AGPL-3.0, with no ads, no account to create and no telemetry. If you'd like to support development, there's an optional PayPal donation link.

Is this an official Google app?

No. It's an independent open-source project that shows the real calendar.google.com in its own window. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Google.

Does it have a dark mode?

Yes. Turn on dark mode in Google Calendar's own settings (the gear icon) and the widget's title bar, frame and hover panel switch to match within a second.

Will it get in the way of my work or games?

No. In widget mode it stays beneath every other window, never takes focus and lets every click through, so anything you open simply covers it.

Windows says the installer is unrecognized. Is it safe?

The installer isn't code-signed, because certificates cost money and this app is free. Click "More info", then "Run anyway". The full source code is on GitHub, so you can review it or build it yourself.

Where is my Google sign-in stored?

Only on your computer, in the app's private browser profile, the same way Chrome stores a session. In Settings you can sign out and keep the account for a quicker sign-in later, or delete all of the widget's browser data. The uninstaller also offers to remove everything.

Does it work with multiple monitors and high-DPI screens?

Yes. Move it to any monitor, snap or maximize it there, and it remembers its place. It stays sharp at every Windows display scale (125 %, 150 %, 200 % and more), including setups that mix monitors with different scales.

What do I need to run it?

Windows 10 or 11 and a Google account. The installer downloads the .NET 8 Desktop Runtime and Microsoft Edge WebView2 only if they're missing (WebView2 is built into Windows 11).

How do I uninstall it?

Like any other app, from Windows Settings → Apps. The uninstaller asks whether to also remove your saved settings and Google sign-in data.
